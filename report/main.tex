\begin{abstract}
This report implements and checks image-framing baselines for SemEval 2027 Task 9, Subtask 2. The available public organizer \emph{trial} archive contains 20 labeled article--image pairs, five in each of Bulgarian, English, European Portuguese, and Russian. We audit the data, produce a separate exploratory-data-analysis (EDA) PDF, and compare handcrafted image features with frozen ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 features. Both representations are evaluated as image-only, image plus article text (2a), and image plus article text plus gold text-frame context (2b) systems. On one grouped split of 14 fit, three calibration, and three evaluation images, all six variants obtain 0.000 provisional micro-F1. This result verifies the pipeline but cannot estimate competition performance: the held-out set contains only two positive image labels, several frames are absent from the fit set, the full training data and official scorer were unavailable, and the task page is ambiguous about scoring the empty-frame case.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Task Definition}\label{sec:task}
A news image can foreground a political actor, a threat, a cultural identity, or another interpretation of the accompanying story. In Subtask 2 of the organizer's task~\cite{semeval2027task9}, the observable inputs are an image and its news article; the output is zero or more \emph{image} frames from 14 generic categories. The target is multilabel, so several frames or no frame are valid. The article is context, not the image's gold answer. Subtask 2a does not provide gold article frames, while 2b does. The task covers Bulgarian (BG), English (EN), European Portuguese (PT), and Russian (RU).

Image framing is difficult because visual cues can be subtle, the same photograph can support multiple interpretations, and article and image frames can disagree. Source templates, embedded text, and repeated images may also create shortcuts. The official primary metric is multilabel micro-F1; Section~\ref{sec:eval} explains the local approximation and its remaining scorer uncertainty. This report describes the 20-example trial archive and does not claim a result on the full training or hidden evaluation sets.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
Card et al.~\cite{card2015} created the Media Frames Corpus, annotating frame cues in US news text across issues. Its taxonomy motivates the present labels, but its text spans and policy-focused news differ from an image target. Piskorski et al.~\cite{piskorski2023} describe SemEval-2023 Task 3, which includes multilingual news framing among three tasks; its framing subtask predicts generic frames in text, so its modeling and evaluation ideas do not directly solve image framing. Arora et al.~\cite{arora2025} explicitly compare frames in news text and images using vision-language models at scale. Their multimodal analysis motivates checking agreement between modalities, but their automatically obtained labels and model setup are distinct from this trial archive. He et al.~\cite{he2016} introduced residual visual networks; we use a frozen ResNet-18 ImageNet encoder as a small pretrained visual baseline. A frozen object-recognition representation may miss abstract framing cues, which is a limitation to test on more data. An organizer baseline paper for the 2027 task was not available with the trial archive.

\section{Exploratory Data Analysis}\label{sec:eda}
The separate \texttt{trial-eda.pdf} contains labeled plots of frame frequency and co-occurrence, image aspect ratio, brightness, saturation, edge density, article word count, language balance, and frame-feature mean differences. The selected groups address label imbalance, image geometry, color and lighting, visual complexity, article context, and language. Frame-feature differences are standardized by the overall feature standard deviation; they are descriptive associations, not causal effects. With one to five positive examples per observed frame, all such patterns are unstable.

\paragraph{Observation.} English articles have a median length of 754 words, versus 270 for Bulgarian and 269 for Russian in this trial. \emph{Interpretation:} source or language collection practices could explain the gap; it does not establish a framing effect. \emph{Modeling implication:} fit article TF-IDF only on training examples and compare image-only results so article length or language cannot be mistaken for visual understanding.

\paragraph{Observation.} Images tagged \emph{Health and safety} are brighter than other images by 1.43 overall-feature standard deviations in the trial; \emph{Crime and punishment} images are darker by 0.92. \emph{Interpretation:} three positive images in each group are too few to infer a general visual pattern, and source or topic may confound the comparison. \emph{Modeling implication:} include simple brightness statistics in the classical baseline, then check whether the association persists on full training data and across sources.

\paragraph{Observation.} The two \emph{Cultural identity} images have higher aspect ratio than other images by 1.07 overall-feature standard deviations. \emph{Interpretation:} this may reflect photo selection or layout rather than the frame itself. \emph{Modeling implication:} retain geometry as a diagnostic feature, but investigate template and source effects before attributing importance to it.

\section{Feature Engineering}\label{sec:features}
The classical representation uses 59 image features: log dimensions, aspect ratio and area; mean and dispersion of grayscale brightness, saturation, and RGB channels; top, bottom, and center brightness; edge statistics; and grayscale/RGB histograms. Every image is converted to RGB with EXIF orientation applied and resized to at most 256 pixels per side for statistics. Article context uses word and bigram TF-IDF with a 20,000-feature cap, learned only from the fit partition. The 2b variant appends a 14-dimensional binary vector of gold text frames aggregated from paragraphs.

The pretrained representation is the 512-dimensional penultimate output of torchvision ResNet-18 with \texttt{IMAGENET1K\_V1} weights~\cite{he2016}. Its published image transforms resize/crop to 224 pixels and normalize channels. The encoder is frozen; only logistic classifier heads are trained. OCR and explicit article--image semantic similarity were not implemented in this first baseline.

\section{Baseline Models}\label{sec:models}
For each of 14 image frames, both representations fit a regularized, class-weighted binary logistic regression head. A frame absent from the fit partition receives a constant head; this is recorded rather than silently dropping the frame. The three input settings are image only, image plus article TF-IDF (2a), and image plus article TF-IDF plus gold text-frame vector (2b). These settings expose whether article context changes errors, without equating article framing with image framing. The 2b trial vector is the union of paragraph frames, a documented approximation to the organizer's eventual 2b input contract.

\section{Evaluation Setup}\label{sec:eval}
We use seed 42 and keep article-linked images together. A search over deterministic grouped splits favors frame and language balance, producing 14 fit, three calibration, and three held-out evaluation images. A shared probability threshold is chosen from 0.10 to 0.90 in steps of 0.05 using calibration micro-F1 only. The evaluation images are \texttt{BG\_TRAIL\_1.jpg}, \texttt{EN\_TRAIL\_3.png}, and \texttt{PT\_doc\_2.jpg}. They contain two positive image labels total and one unframed image. The fit split has five constant heads because their frames have no positive examples.

For aggregate true positives $TP$, false positives $FP$, and false negatives $FN$, micro-F1 is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:micro}
F_{1,\mathrm{micro}}=\frac{2TP}{2TP+FP+FN}.
\end{equation}
Macro-F1 averages the 14 per-frame F1 values, including frames unsupported in the tiny held-out partition. Per-frame precision and recall are saved with every run. The organizer's Subtask 2 page mentions a \texttt{None} label while referring to \emph{no framing in text}, so its exact image scorer behavior remains unverified. Our provisional local score excludes \texttt{None} as a positive class but counts predictions on unframed images as false positives. No organizer development or test labels were used to choose thresholds.

Table~\ref{tab:results} reports the single held-out trial split. Every variant has zero true positives and thus zero micro- and macro-F1. \section{Error Analysis}\label{sec:errors}
In the image-only evaluation, the classical model predicts Health and safety plus Political for the Security and defense image \texttt{BG\_TRAIL\_1.jpg}. It assigns Security and defense plus Public opinion to the unframed \texttt{EN\_TRAIL\_3.png}. For the Cultural identity image \texttt{PT\_doc\_2.jpg}, it predicts five other frames. The frozen encoder leaves the first two images unframed, correctly avoiding a false positive on the English image but missing the Bulgarian positive label; it predicts Crime and punishment for the Portuguese image. Cultural identity cannot be learned by either head in this split because it is absent from the fit partition. The saved per-image probability CSVs permit further inspection without using the hidden development set.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
The importer, audit, plotting, six model configurations, scoring, and response writer run end to end on actual organizer trial files. The reported zero F1 chiefly exposes the limits of this sample and split: four labels do not occur at all, three occur once, five heads are constant after splitting, and the evaluation contains only two positive labels. Gold text frames differ from image frame sets for all 20 pairs, so 2b context can also mislead. Brightness, article-length, and aspect-ratio patterns are hypotheses for checking on the full set, not established findings. The frozen ImageNet representation is a reasonable baseline, but its object categories need not align with abstract frames. The current training command saves heads fit on 14 examples; final organizer submissions should be refit on all permitted labeled training examples after model selection.

\section*{Reproducibility}
From the repository root, download the public trial ZIP, then run \texttt{python -m framing prepare-trial --archive data/trial.zip --out data}. Next run \texttt{python -m framing audit} and \texttt{python -m framing eda} with \texttt{--manifest data/manifest.csv --labels data/framing-labels.txt} and distinct \texttt{--out} directories. Run \texttt{python -m framing train} with the same manifest and label arguments, \texttt{--model classical} or \texttt{--model frozen --checkpoint torchvision:resnet18}, \texttt{--enable-2b --seed 42 --none-mode exclude}, and distinct run folders. Install \texttt{requirements.txt} for the classical system and \texttt{requirements-pretrained.txt} for the frozen system. The run folders store split assignments, package versions, configuration, held-out metrics, probabilities, and models. The trial importer records the archive digest and mapping rule in \texttt{data/trial-provenance.json}. The frozen run used torch 2.5.1+cpu and torchvision 0.20.1+cpu; classical dependencies were installed from the project requirement file. Hardware was a CPU-only cloud runner.
